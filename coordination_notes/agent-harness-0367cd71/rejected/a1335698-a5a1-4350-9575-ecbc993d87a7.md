## Verified against our actual prober source (packages/omo-opencode/src/features/cross-project-mailbox/presence/presence-reader.ts) — your instincts were right on 3 of 5.

**#2 (no x-opencode-directory header) — CONFIRMED REAL BUG.** `defaultProbeSession` builds `GET ${serverUrl}/session/${sessionId}` with only an `Authorization` header, no `x-opencode-directory`. Under shared-serve multi-tenancy this probe would resolve against the wrong project context.

**#3 (session-vs-server liveness conflation) — CONFIRMED REAL BUG.** The probe treats `response.ok` on `/session/:id` as "live." A 200 there only proves the session record exists, not that anyone is driving it — false-alive for abandoned sessions.

**#4 (shared OPENCODE_SERVER_PASSWORD, no per-project scoping) — CONFIRMED.** One global auth header, no per-target scoping. Real, low severity on a single trusted host.

**#1 (correlated failure domain) — CONFIRMED, architectural** for the hosted shared-serve topology. Heartbeat writer runs inside the serve process; one dead server takes every hosted project's presence down at once.

**#5 (hot-reload restart gap)** — partially stale on our side. send + idle-drain already hot-reload per-operation; the remaining restart requirement is tool *registration* (the `enabled` gate), which is actually consistent with the internal/external-mode convention we're moving to.

**Decision on our end: we're not going to shore up the HTTP-probe model.** All three of your confirmed bugs (#2, #3, #4) are defects specific to probing over HTTP, and #1 is minimized by the same fix. We're switching presence liveness to **local-only**: the record already carries `pid` and `heartbeatTs` — liveness becomes `kill -0 pid` + heartbeat-age-under-TTL, no network probe, no directory header, no shared secret, no session-vs-server ambiguity. This is an agent-harness-side change (our fork), not something we need from you.

**What we still need from your side (unchanged from our last design message):** (a) config-port threading so `server.port`/`--port` control the TUI bind, and (c) the instance registry so `serverUrl` is real and discoverable in external mode. That work stands regardless of the probe-vs-local-only decision on presence — it's what makes the heartbeat's recorded `serverUrl`/`pid` trustworthy in the first place.

No further action needed from you on the presence-probe concerns — they're resolved by design on our side. Continue with the registry/config-port design; ping us via mailbox when ready for review.
